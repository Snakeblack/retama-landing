## What it includes

- **A technical audit.** We review the speed, titles, links and languages of your website, its sitemap (the list of pages Google reads) and its structured data, labels that tell Google what each thing is: a price, an address, an article.
- **An improvement plan, in order.** What to fix first and why. What holds Google back comes before keywords: slow pages, broken links and whatever does not get indexed.
- **Cookie-free analytics, installed.** You know which pages are read, where visits come from and how long each page takes to load. Visits are counted in aggregate, without a profile of each visitor.

## What drives the price

The final price is set by the proposal, which is free. It goes up or down mostly with these:

- **The size of the website.** Reviewing a ten-page website takes less than a shop with thousands of products.
- **The languages.** Each language has its own pages, and Google has to know which one to show to each person.
- **The starting point.** If the website has just changed its address or its design, it may have lost visits on the way, and finding out where takes time.

## Why there is no fee

A technical audit has a beginning and an end: we hand over the audit and the plan in writing, with the analytics already installed, and from there you decide. With the analytics you can see for yourself whether visits go up after each improvement, without waiting for a monthly report from us. Monthly content SEO is a different job, and we do not do it.

## What nobody can promise you

Google says so in its own guide to hiring an SEO: [no one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/do-i-need-seo). If someone guarantees you a position, find someone else. According to the same guide, an audit should give you a realistic estimate of the improvements and of the work they take.

## What to ask before you hire

Google's guide has a list of questions for the SEO. These help you compare any proposal:

- What results they expect, in what timeframe and how they will measure them.
- Whether they will tell you about every change they make to your website, and why.
- What access they need. For a review, being able to read your Search Console data, Google's free tool, is enough, with no permission to change anything.
