An AI agent or an automation takes over a job your team repeats every day or every week: moving the orders that arrive by email into your system, preparing the Monday report or warning you when something is out of the ordinary. What it costs depends on how many systems it touches and how many exceptions the job has, but you can know what makes up the price before you ask for a quote.

## Agent or automation

An automation follows fixed rules: when your usual customer sends the order in their usual PDF, it copies each field into your ERP. An agent also uses an AI model to read what does not always arrive in the same shape, such as an order typed into the body of an email, and proposes what to do with it. Many projects mix the two, and both start from the same price. Only the agent adds the cost of using the model.

## What drives the price of the set-up

- **How many systems it talks to.** Reading one mailbox is simple. Reading the email, checking stock in your ERP and noting it in a shared spreadsheet are three connections that have to be built and tested.
- **How many exceptions the job has.** One customer's order format is solved quickly; thirty customers, each with their own, take longer.
- **What a person has to approve.** Anything that cannot be undone, such as an order to a supplier or an email to a customer, is confirmed by someone on your team before it goes out, and building that step is work too.
- **What happens when it fails.** Every step is logged, so that if something goes wrong you know why and since when. The more alerts and checks your case needs, the more work it takes.

## What the monthly fee covers

An agent works with parts you do not control: the format of the emails it receives, the systems it connects to and the AI model it uses. Any of them can change without warning, and then the agent stops doing its job well until someone adjusts it. The fee, from €150 a month, pays for it to keep running and for someone to keep an eye on it. It is paid monthly, for three months at least. Taking it to another task is a separate project, with its own price.

## Who pays for the AI

Every time the agent reads an email or a document, it queries an AI model, and the model's provider charges for that use. You pay it, from your own account with the provider and at their price, with no margin of ours: you see each month what it spends, and the account is yours. The proposal estimates that cost from the volume of work in your case. An automation that uses no AI model has no such expense.

## If it talks to your customers

If the agent answers emails or handles a chat, the EU AI Act requires that whoever talks to it knows they are talking to an AI, unless that is obvious ([Article 50](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689#art_50)). That notice is part of what we build.

## How to tell whether it pays off

Do the sums with your own figures: the hours your team spends on that task each week, times what each hour costs you, times the weeks in a year. Compare that with the set-up, twelve fees and your estimate of the model's use. The proposal gives you that sum done, with the savings in euros.

And start with one task: we measure the savings before we scale, and we only automate more of what has already given you hours back.
